## Why

After the log gating (async-log-writer, merged 2026-10-14) mission 1-2 still runs slow on native Windows 11 (Ryzen 7 3700X, RTX 2060): on the merged build (`runs/asynclog/win/game-20261014-164545.log`, render.scale=3, `RECOMP_TRACE=pacing,apu`) the city-facing windows pace at p50 38.4-41.8 ms and p95 40.2-45.3 ms against the 33.3 ms lock, with process CPU at 187-199%. The slow windows are the ones with more than about 6000 draws a frame, at render scale 1 as much as at 3 (BLX-100, 2026-10-14). Proton on the Deck and the Linux/Proton host, and macOS Metal, hold 30.

The WPR capture of that view (118 s; `runs/asynclog/win/prof-threads.txt`, `prof-walker-waits.txt`) says where the time goes:

- The guest main thread (tid 12624, 49% of the samples) is 93% in the exe, 82% of it on two instructions: the D3D KickOff spin at guest 0x2E7F7A. It is waiting, not working.
- The walker thread (tid 10260, `nv2a-ack` in toolkit `src/kernel/xbox_memory_layout.c`) is busy only 48% of the span. The other 52% (61.4 s) it spends in `DelayExecution`: 79,637 sleeps, about 680 a second, 0.77 ms each on average, 78,743 of them readied by Idle, that is by the timer. The 560 `WrResource` and 191 `UserRequest` waits are the D3D11 runtime's own and amount to under 1%.

The sleeps are the ack loop's naps. With `RECOMP_FAST_KICK` (default on) the walker polls the KickOff flush bit and `DMA_PUT` every ~50 us between its 1 ms ticks (`xbox_memory_layout.c:1886-1927`), so that a kick is acknowledged as soon as the segment before it is executed and a new `PUT` is picked up at once. `kick_nap_us(50)` (line 1556) asks for that with `NtDelayExecution(-500)`, 100 ns units. Wine honours it: its non-alertable delay is a `select` with a timeval. Native Windows rounds a delay up to the timer resolution, which is 1 ms once the kernel timer thread has called `timeBeginPeriod(1)` (`kernel_bridge.c:3750`). So on Windows every poll sleeps about a millisecond, the title's KickOff waits up to a millisecond for an ack that costs 50 us elsewhere, and the walker starts a new segment up to a millisecond late. At 30 fps that is 22 naps a frame, 17 ms of the walker's 38-42 ms frame asleep; the kick count rises with the draw count, which is why the slowdown follows draws a frame and not render scale.

The first version of this change (toolkit 2f22039) moved the nap to the high-resolution waitable timer and expected it to wake within tens of microseconds. Measured on the user's PC, 200 naps of 50 us: `NtDelayExecution` p50 1005 us, the high-resolution timer p50 511 us (p95 542), a spin on the clock p50 50 us. The timer halves the nap; it does not fix it, because every timed wait on Windows expires on the system clock interrupt and the finest clock this hardware offers is 0.5 ms (`notes/kicknap/windows-sub-ms-waits.md`: the WDK's own statement, Bruce Dawson's 2020 table with the same 535-585 us for 10-100 us requests, and every emulator and frame limiter that needs better spinning the last clock interval). Nothing in user mode wakes a *timed* wait earlier: not `NtSetTimerResolution(5000)`, not MMCSS, not thread priority, not `SwitchToThread` (which returns at once on an idle core and gives a whole quantum away on a busy one). Below 0.5 ms Windows offers two things: a spin, or a wake from a thread that knows the event happened.

Nothing else on the walker waits sub-millisecond: the D3D11 backend polls its queries with `GetData` and never sleeps (`src/d3d/nv2a_pb_d3d11.c`), the flip hold and the fences are polled in the same loop, and the idle `Sleep(1)` paths (line 1883, 1921) are meant as a millisecond. The observer thread that times kicks for `[PB-PERF]` (`nv2a_kick_thread`, line 1606, under `RECOMP_TRACE=flip`) naps through the same helper, so on Windows it also stamps a kick up to 1 ms late and `kickwait` under-reads there. The guest's side of the hand-off is already event-driven: the lowered KickOff spin (site 0x002E7F20, `config/spin_waits.json`) sleeps on a condition variable that `kick_cleared` wakes through `xbox_SpinWake` (`kernel_pacing.c:156`, `248`).

## What Changes

- **The nap is a bounded hybrid on native Windows** (design.md D1). `kick_nap_us` becomes a call to a host helper in `src/platform/host_time.c`, `xbox_HostNapNs`, with three primitives: the thread's high-resolution waitable timer (`CREATE_WAITABLE_TIMER_HIGH_RESOLUTION`, Windows 10 1803+, the timer `xbox_HostSleepNs` already keeps per thread), today's `NtDelayExecution` / `nanosleep`, and a `pause` spin bounded by the clock. On native Windows the ack loop spins while it is within a 2 ms budget of the last activity (a kick acknowledged, a new `PUT`, a flip hold) and takes the timer past it; the 200 ms idle `Sleep(1)` is unchanged. Under Wine the nap keeps `NtDelayExecution` exactly as today (a `select` that honours 50 us), on POSIX `nanosleep`. Wine is detected once by ntdll's `wine_get_version` export (`xbox_HostUnderWine`). Where the high-resolution timer cannot be created the cold path falls back to `NtDelayExecution` and the startup line says so. The nap mode is set before the ack thread exists (`kick_nap_init`).
- **The startup line measures the nap** (D2): `NV2A KickOff flush: ...; nap hybrid (spin within 2 ms of activity, then timer): spin p50 50 us, timer p50 511 us`, from 32 naps of each primitive at start, so a Windows log states the floor the host actually gives.
- **One A/B knob** (D3): `RECOMP_DEBUG=kick_nap=hybrid|timer|delay|spin`, DEBUG tier, with a `docs/env.md` row. Unset is the rule above (`hybrid` on native Windows, `delay` under Wine and on POSIX); `timer` on Windows is the first version of this fix and `delay` the old path, both for the recorded A/B; `spin` is the unbounded spin, the floor measurement.
- **Windows 11 keeps the 1 ms clock when the window is occluded** (D5): next to `timeBeginPeriod(1)` the kernel timer thread opts the process out of `PROCESS_POWER_THROTTLING_IGNORE_TIMER_RESOLUTION`, so the idle `Sleep(1)` paths and the ms vblank schedule stay at 1 ms when the game window loses focus.
- **A standalone ctest** `tests/host_nap` (D4): builds `host_time.c` alone, times 200 naps of 50 us per primitive, bounds the spin (and so the hybrid's hot path) and the Wine/POSIX default, and prints the timer and the delay for the record; runs under `tests/posix_ctest.sh` (it picks up every standalone dir) and, once the CLI's Proton list carries it, under `blinx2 bench tests`. The same exe run by hand on native Windows records the 0.5 ms timer floor and the 1 ms `NtDelayExecution` beside the 50 us spin.

Not in this change: the idle `Sleep(1)` paths (a millisecond by design), the 1 ms tick itself, the guest's spin lowering, and the event-driven kick leg (a "doorbell" rung from the lowered KickOff spin site, which would remove the 50 us poll on every host; a separate follow-up, design.md Non-Goals). Also out of scope, pre-existing: `nv2a_pb_now_ns` on Windows reads `timespec_get(TIME_UTC)`, a clock that is not monotonic; the loop's `last_active` arithmetic inherits that until it moves to `xbox_HostNowNs`.

## Impact

- **Toolkit** (`posix-host/portability`, from 38717b2):
  - `src/platform/host_time.{c,h}`: `xbox_HostUnderWine`, `xbox_HostNapNs`, `xbox_HostNapHotNs`, `xbox_HostNapModeSet`, `xbox_HostNapMode`.
  - `src/kernel/xbox_memory_layout.c`: `kick_nap_init` (mode, measurement, startup line) before the ack thread starts; `kick_nap_us` on the helper with the hot/cold choice from `last_active`; the observer thread on the cold primitive.
  - `src/kernel/kernel_bridge.c`: the power-throttling opt-out beside `timeBeginPeriod(1)`.
  - `src/platform/recomp_env.h`: the `KICK_NAP` row.
  - `tests/host_nap/`: the ctest.
- **CLI**: `bench/host/tests.sh` and `bench/__init__.py`: `host_nap` on the optional standalone list (skipped when a game's toolkit pin predates it). Its own small commit; cat's `[cli]` pin moves at the next CLI tag, so until then the Deck gate runs the test by hand (tasks.md 2.2).
- **Cat**: `docs/env.md` (the row), TASKS.md, `analysis/upstream/pr-stack-plan.md`.
- **Hosts and backends**: native Windows is the only host whose behaviour changes; Wine/Proton and macOS run the same wait calls as today, so the D3D11 goldens under Proton and the Metal goldens are unchanged by construction and are run as the check. The walker is shared by every backend (CPU, D3D11, Metal), so they agree by construction; Burnout 3 runs under Proton on the exact toolkit head.
- **Performance**: on native Windows the walker's naps go from about 0.77 ms to ~50 us while the title is kicking and to the timer's 0.5 ms floor only in gaps longer than the 2 ms budget. Expected result on the 1-2 city view: p50 back at 33.3 ms, p95 within 1-1's band (34-37 ms). The cost is the walker's core busy for the active part of the frame (the 52% of wall time it spent asleep becomes a `pause` spin): `[PACING]`'s `cpu process` is expected to rise by roughly 50 percentage points on the 3700X, and the WPR capture shows the walker's wait count collapsing. In menus and light scenes the loop is back on the timer.
- **Upstream**: yes. `host_time.c` lands with PR 4 (pacing: the vblank schedule runs on it), so the helper, the Wine check and the power-throttling opt-out go there; the ack loop's use of it and the hybrid policy go with G2 (the walker from GET to PUT), which already carries `RECOMP_FAST_KICK`; the knob is `getenv` in the PR. The `tests/host_nap` ctest goes with PR 4.
